# Venice: facts for review

**Approved by Dan, 15 October 2026.** The facts are in `src/facts.js`; the city is in the release queue and no player sees it until it is released.

3 streets × 3 facts, 3 monuments × 3 facts.

## Approach

- Facts are architectural and historical, with the water at the centre: canals, bridges, gondolas, the tide.
- The plague is named once, as the reason the Salute was built (its name means "health"). No detail beyond that.
- **No height graphics.** None of the three monuments has a height worth comparing, so each page shows its facts alone.
- Gondolas in the game are plain black boats with a gondolier, with no club or company markings.

## Streets

### 1. Strada Nova: *the new street*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1871**, opened | The Strada Nova, the "new street", opened in 1871, cut through old houses to make one walk from the Rialto to the railway station. |
| **472 bridges**, in the city | Venice is built on 126 islands, joined by 472 bridges. |
| *(none)* | There are no cars in the old city. Goods go round on porters' trolleys, and by boat. |

### 2. Zattere: *the long quay*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1 km**, of quay | The Zattere runs for about a kilometre along the Giudecca Canal, to the point where it meets the basin of San Marco. |
| *(none)* | Its name means rafts: salt, timber and coal were once floated down the canal on rafts and landed here. |
| **400 gondolas**, today | In the 1500s Venice had about 10,000 gondolas and boats like them. Today there are about 400 gondolas. |

The name: Italian Wikipedia gives the rafts as the more reliable of two explanations; the fact gives that one only.

### 3. Riva degli Schiavoni: *the waterfront*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | It is named after the merchants of Dalmatia, then called Schiavonia, who landed their cargo here from across the Adriatic. |
| **194 cm**, highest tide | At high tide, the acqua alta, the lagoon floods the lowest streets. The highest on record, 194 cm, came on 4 November 1966. |
| **2020**, flood gates | Since 2020, gates on the seabed at the mouths of the lagoon can rise to hold back the highest tides. |

## Monuments

### Rialto Bridge

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1591**, completed | The stone bridge was built from 1588 to 1591, to a design by Antonio da Ponte. |
| *(none)* | It replaced a wooden bridge and kept its shape: one arch, with rows of shops along it. |
| *(none)* | It is the oldest of the four bridges across the Grand Canal. |

### Santa Maria della Salute

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1631**, begun | Venice vowed to build it in 1630, in thanks for the end of a terrible plague. Work began in 1631. |
| **56 years**, to build | It was finished in 1687. Its architect, Baldassare Longhena, died five years before. |
| *(none)* | Every year on 21 November a bridge on floats is laid across the Grand Canal, so people can walk to it. |

### Doge's Palace

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1340**, rebuilt | A palace stood here from the 800s. The building you see was begun in 1340 and added to over the next two centuries. |
| *(none)* | It was the home of the Doge, Venice's ruler, and the seat of its government. It has been a museum since 1923. |
| **1600**, Bridge of Sighs | The enclosed Bridge of Sighs, built around 1600, led from the palace's courtrooms to the prison. Lord Byron gave it its English name. |

## Points that were open at approval

- "No cars in the old city; goods go round on porters' trolleys and by boat": well known, not in the sources below.
- "Gates at the mouths of the lagoon": the MOSE source names the Lido inlet; the barrier closes all three inlets (Lido, Malamocco, Chioggia), which the fact does not count.

## Copyright

All three monuments are long out of copyright: the Rialto (1591), the Salute (1687), the Doge's Palace (1340 onwards) and the Bridge of Sighs (about 1600). The Campanile on the Riva's skyline is the 1912 rebuilding of the 16th-century tower to its old design, not a new design.
