# Chicago: facts for review

**Approved by Dan, 13 October 2026**, including the points under "To check before approval" below, which he checked and accepted. The facts are in `src/facts.js`; the city is in the release queue and no player sees it until it is released.

3 streets × 3 facts, 3 monuments × 3 facts.

## Approach

- Facts are architectural and historical.
- **"Magnificent Mile" is a registered trademark** (of The Magnificent Mile Association). The street is called Michigan Avenue in the game, and the nickname is not used anywhere.
- The Willis Tower is named after a company. Naming the building by its official name in a fact is ordinary descriptive use; it is not used as a brand, and the old name (the name of a retailer) is not used. Its shape is not a registered mark that I could find (unlike the Transamerica Pyramid's).
- The Bean, the Picasso and the Miró are not in the game (living or recent artists' sculptures). Buckingham Fountain's bronze sea horses are not modelled either; their pedestals stand empty.

## Streets

### 1. Michigan Avenue: *north of the river*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | This stretch of Michigan Avenue runs about a mile north from the Chicago River, lined with shops, hotels and towers. |
| *(none)* | It was once a narrow road called Pine Street. It grew into a grand avenue after a double-decked bridge opened over the river in 1920. |
| **1920**, the bridge | That bridge is a bascule bridge: its two halves tip up to let tall boats through. |

### 2. The Loop: *under the tracks*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1897**, the Loop opened | In 1897 the elevated railway's loop of track opened over the streets of downtown Chicago. |
| *(none)* | The tracks run round a rectangle of streets, and that loop gave the whole downtown its name: the Loop. |
| *(none)* | It is one of only a few city railways that have kept running, day in, day out, for well over a century. |

### 3. Wacker Drive: *along the river*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | Wacker Drive runs along the Chicago River, and has a second road on a lower level beneath it. |
| **37 bridges**, that move | Chicago has 37 bridges that open to let boats through, by most counts more than any other city in the world. |
| *(none)* | Most are bascule bridges, a design so common here that it is called the 'Chicago type'. |

## Monuments

### Chicago Water Tower (scale: 47 m, compared with the Willis Tower; note: "Chicago's tallest building is over nine times taller.")

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1869**, built | The Water Tower was finished in 1869 to hide a tall iron pipe that helped pump water to the city. |
| **1871**, the Great Fire | It was the only public building in the burned area to survive the Great Chicago Fire of 1871. |
| **47 m**, tall | It stands 47 metres tall, built of yellow limestone. |

"Only public building" is deliberate: a few other buildings survived, so "only building" would be wrong.

### Buckingham Fountain (scale: 46 m, the height of its jet, compared with a person)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1927**, opened | Buckingham Fountain opened in 1927, one of the largest fountains in the world. |
| **46 m**, the centre jet | In its big display, the centre jet shoots water 46 metres into the air. |
| *(none)* | The water show runs for 20 minutes every hour, from spring to autumn. |

The scale compares the jet's height (150 ft), not the fountain's.

### Willis Tower (scale: 442 m, compared with the Empire State Building; note: "Each was the world's tallest building in its day." Empire State 1931 to 1970, Willis 1973 to 1998)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **442 m**, tall | The tower rises 442 metres to its roof, more with its antennas. |
| **1973**, opened | When it opened in 1973 it was the tallest building in the world, and it held that title for nearly 25 years. |
| *(none)* | It is built as nine square tubes bundled together. They stop at different heights, so the tower steps back as it rises. |

## To check before approval

- "One of the largest fountains in the world": a common description, not in the sources below.
- Wacker Drive's lower level and the "Chicago type" name: well known; the second is in the Chicago Architecture Center source.

## Copyright

All three were finished before 1 December 1990, so US copyright does not cover them as buildings.
