## Approach

- Thai law makes it a crime to insult the monarchy. The facts name kings only as builders, with dates, and say nothing else about them or the royal family.
- Wat Arun is an active temple. It is shown as a building: no Buddha images in the monument or the puzzle pieces, and the facts are about its construction.
- Khao San Road is known for nightlife; the facts and signs stay with its history as a rice market and with food and guesthouses.

## Streets

### 1. Yaowarat Road: *Bangkok's Chinatown*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1.5 km**, long | Yaowarat Road runs about one and a half kilometres through the heart of Bangkok's Chinatown. |
| **1891**, ordered | King Chulalongkorn ordered the road built in 1891, over land that had been rice fields and canals. |
| *(none)* | It bends because it was laid out around the homes already there. People say it looks like the body of a dragon. |

### 2. Khao San Road: *the rice street*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **410 m**, long | Khao San Road is only about 410 metres long. |
| *(none)* | Its name means 'milled rice': by the end of the 1800s this district was the biggest rice market in the country. |
| *(none)* | Rice came up the Chao Phraya River on barges and was unloaded at a canal nearby. |

### 3. Khlong Bang Luang: *life by the canal*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | Bangkok was once called the 'Venice of the East', because canals, called khlongs, crossed the whole city. |
| **1,682**, canals | In 2019 the city counted 1,682 canals, with 2,604 kilometres of waterways between them. |
| *(none)* | Many canals were filled in to make roads, but in places like Bang Luang, wooden houses still stand right on the water. |

## Monuments

### Giant Swing (scale: 21 m, compared with a person)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **21 m**, tall | The Giant Swing stands 21 metres tall, in front of the Devasathan shrine. |
| **1784**, first built | It was first built in 1784. Its great teak pillars were replaced, to the same design, in 2007. |
| *(none)* | A swinging ceremony was once held here every year. It stopped in 1935. |

Date: most sources say 1784; one says 1786. The ceremony stopped after fatal accidents; the fact leaves out the cause.

### Wat Arun (scale: 82 m, compared with a bus)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **82 m**, the central spire | Its central tower, the prang, rises about 82 metres. |
| **1851**, completed | The central prang was finished in 1851, after building began under King Rama II and went on under King Rama III. |
| *(none)* | Its tiers are covered in broken porcelain and seashells. Some of the china came to Bangkok as ballast in ships from China. |

82 m is Dan's figure. Wikipedia gives 66.8 to 86 m; travel guides often say 82 m.

### Chakri Maha Prasat (no height graphic)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1882**, completed | The throne hall was finished in 1882, a hundred years after the Grand Palace was founded. |
| *(none)* | Its body is built in a European style, but its three roofs are traditional Thai, each crowned with a tall spire. |
| **1782**, palace founded | The Grand Palace was begun in 1782, the year Bangkok became the capital. |

No height or altitude: the page shows the three facts alone.
