# Marrakech: facts for review

**DRAFT. Nothing here ships until Dan has approved it.** The facts are in `src/facts.js` already, marked DRAFT, so the city can be played and tested; the city is in the release queue and no player sees it.

Same structure as every city: 3 streets × 3 facts, 3 monuments × 3 facts. A fact may carry a statistic that animates on screen.

## Approach

- Facts are architectural and historical: dates, sizes, materials, what a place was used for.
- The Koutoubia is an active mosque. The facts describe the minaret as a building.
- The Mellah is described as the old Jewish quarter, with its founding date and its community's long history. Its later emigration is left out as not needed for a children's game; say if you want it in.

## Streets

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|

| *(none)* | Storytellers still gather their listeners in a circle called a halqa and tell tales that have been passed on by word of mouth for centuries. |

## Monuments

### Koutoubia Minaret (scale: 77 m, compared with a bus)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **77 m**, tall with its spire | The minaret rises 77 metres, spire and all, and can be seen from almost anywhere in the old city. |
| **1195**, finished around | It was completed around 1195 under Caliph Yaqub al-Mansur, and it has stood almost unchanged ever since. |
| *(none)* | Its name means the booksellers' mosque: booksellers once kept their stalls all around it. |

Height: 77 m includes the 8 m spire (Wikipedia). Some guides give 68–69 m, which is the tower without the spire. The fact says "with its spire" so both are consistent.

### Bab Agnaou (no sourced height: shows altitude, 466 m)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1188**, built around | Caliph Yaqub al-Mansur built the gate around 1188 as the grand entrance to his royal kasbah. |
| *(none)* | Most of the city's gates are rammed red earth, but Bab Agnaou is cut stone, carved with an inscription and flowers around its horseshoe arch. |
| *(none)* | It once had two towers and a covered passage behind it. Those are gone, and the carved front is what still stands. |

Date: Wikipedia gives 1188 or 1190; Archnet credits an earlier gate on the site to Abd al-Mu'min in 1147. "Around 1188" follows the better-supported account.

### Ben Youssef Madrasa (no sourced height: shows altitude, 466 m)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1565**, rebuilt | Sultan Abdallah al-Ghalib rebuilt the school in 1564 and 1565, on the site of a 14th-century one. |
| **130 rooms**, for students | Its 130 small rooms housed up to 800 students, making it the largest madrasa in Morocco. |
| *(none)* | It closed as a school in 1960 and opened to visitors in 1982. Its courtyard is covered in carved cedar, carved plaster and coloured tile. |

Students: Wikipedia says up to 800; History Hit says up to 900. The draft uses Wikipedia's figure.

## To check before approval

- Lazama Synagogue: that it is open to visitors comes from travel guides, not a primary source.
- "One for slippers, one for lanterns": the souks are named by trade (Frommers lists the wool and sheepskin souks); the slipper and lantern souks are named in guides but not in the sources below.
