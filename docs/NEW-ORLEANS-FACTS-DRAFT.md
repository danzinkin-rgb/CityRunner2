# New Orleans: facts for review

**DRAFT. Nothing here ships until Dan has approved it.** The facts are in `src/facts.js` already, marked DRAFT, so the city can be played and tested; the city is in the release queue and no player sees it.

3 streets × 3 facts, 3 monuments × 3 facts.

## Approach

- Facts are architectural and historical, plus Mardi Gras and the streetcars.
- **Left out:** Bourbon Street (drinking), Hurricane Katrina, voodoo shops, and the Andrew Jackson statue in Jackson Square (his record is contested and its removal has been called for). The statue is not modelled either.
- Parade floats are generic: purple, green and gold, a jester's head and a crown. No krewe's float, name or emblem.
- No height graphics: none of the three monuments has a height worth comparing.

## Streets

### 1. Royal Street: *iron lace and antiques*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1718**, city founded | New Orleans was founded in 1718 and grew around the square now called Jackson Square. Royal Street was laid out in its first years. |
| **80%**, burned | Great fires in 1788 and 1794 destroyed 80% of the city, so nearly all of the French Quarter was built from the late 1790s on, much of it under Spanish rule. |
| *(none)* | Royal Street is known for its antique shops and art galleries. |

### 2. St. Charles Avenue: *the parade route*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1835**, streetcar | The St. Charles streetcar has run since 1835: the oldest streetcar line still running in the world. |
| *(none)* | It is one of the main Mardi Gras parade routes, lined with mansions and live oaks planted in the early 1900s. |
| **1857**, the first floats | In 1857 the Mistick Krewe of Comus brought torch-lit processions and themed floats to Mardi Gras. |

### 3. Decatur Street: *along the river*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | Decatur Street runs beside the Mississippi. It was once Levee Street, and was renamed in 1870, after the river had shifted its course. |
| **1791**, the market | Part of the French Market has been a market since 1791. Its oldest buildings date from about 1813. |
| **1988**, riverfront line | The Riverfront streetcar line opened in 1988, the first new streetcar line in the city in 62 years. |

## Monuments

### The Cabildo

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1799**, completed | Built from 1795 to 1799, it was the seat of the Spanish colonial city government. |
| **1803**, Louisiana Purchase | The ceremonies that handed Louisiana to the United States took place here in 1803. |
| **1847**, the roof | Its Spanish arches are topped by a French mansard roof, added in 1847. |

### Pontalba Buildings

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1851**, completed | Baroness Micaela Almonester de Pontalba built the two matching red-brick rows from 1849 to 1851. |
| *(none)* | Her initials, AP, are worked into the cast-iron railings of the balconies. |
| *(none)* | They face each other across Jackson Square, with shops below and homes above. |

The source says the initials are in the first-floor balustrade; the fact says "the balconies".

### St. Louis Cathedral

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1718**, first church | The first church on this site was built in 1718. |
| **1850**, rebuilt | The cathedral was largely rebuilt and completed in the 1850s. |
| *(none)* | It is among the oldest cathedrals in continuous use in the United States. |

Wikipedia says the oldest "alongside the Royal Presidio Chapel in Monterey", so the fact says "among the oldest".

## To check before approval

- "They face each other across Jackson Square": the two buildings stand on opposite sides of the square; the source says matching and one block long but not "facing".

## Copyright

All three buildings are long out of copyright (1795–1799, 1849–1851, 1850s), and US copyright does not cover buildings finished before 1 December 1990 in any case.
