# Hanoi: facts for review

**DRAFT. Nothing here ships until Dan has approved it.** The facts are in `src/facts.js` already, marked DRAFT, so the city can be played and tested; the city is in the release queue and no player sees it.

3 streets × 3 facts, 3 monuments × 3 facts.

## Approach

- Facts are architectural, historical and about daily life. Nothing political: no war history (Long Bien Bridge was bombed and repaired; left out), and the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum is not in the city.
- **Monument change from the plan:** the One Pillar Pagoda was swapped for **Long Bien Bridge**. The pagoda standing today is a 1955 rebuild by the architect Nguyễn Bá Lăng, who died this century, and Vietnam's term is life + 50, so the rebuild could still be protected. Long Bien Bridge (1903) is clear, and suits Train Street: it is a railway bridge.

## Streets

### 1. Hang Gai: *the silk street*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | Hanoi's Old Quarter is called the 36 Streets. Each street was named after the trade of the guild that worked there. |
| *(none)* | 'Hang' means goods. Hang Gai was a centre of the silk trade, and it is still known for silk and tailors. |
| *(none)* | The number 36 is not exact: the Old Quarter has more streets than that today. |

### 2. Train Street: *houses beside the rails*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1902**, railway built | The railway through here was built in 1902, and trains still run along it. |
| *(none)* | The houses stand so close to the rails that people step back into their doorways when a train goes by. |
| *(none)* | In 2019 the cafés along the tracks were closed to visitors for safety. They reopened in 2023 under stricter rules. |

### 3. Hoan Kiem Lake: *the lake of the returned sword*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | Hoan Kiem means 'lake of the returned sword'. |
| **1428**, the legend | The story goes that in 1428 a golden turtle rose from the lake and took back the magic sword Emperor Le Loi had used to win his war. |
| *(none)* | Before the legend, the lake was called Luc Thuy, 'green water'. |

## Monuments

### Turtle Tower (no height graphic)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1886**, built | The little tower was built on its island in the lake in 1886. |
| *(none)* | It is named for the golden turtle of the lake's legend. |

### Long Bien Bridge (no height graphic)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1903**, opened | Designed by the Paris firm Daydé & Pillé, it opened in 1903 as the first steel bridge across the Red River. |
| **2,290 m**, long | It is 2,290 metres long and crosses the river on 19 spans. |
| *(none)* | It was first named after Paul Doumer, the French governor-general, and was renamed Long Bien after 1954. |

Length: sources range from 1,680 m to 2,500 m; 2,290 m (Wikipedia, VietnamNet) is the most common.

### Temple of Literature (no height graphic)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1070**, founded | The temple was founded in 1070, and in 1076 Vietnam's first national university opened beside it. |
| **82 tablets**, on stone turtles | Eighty-two stone tablets, each carried on the back of a stone turtle, record the scholars who passed the royal exams. |
| **1805**, the pavilion | Its Constellation of Literature Pavilion was built in 1805, and it has become a symbol of Hanoi. |
