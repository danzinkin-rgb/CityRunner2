# Delhi: facts for review

**DRAFT. Nothing here ships until Dan has approved it.** The facts are in `src/facts.js` already, marked DRAFT, so the city can be played and tested; the city is in the release queue and no player sees it.

3 streets × 3 facts, 3 monuments × 3 facts.

## Approach

- Facts are architectural and historical.
- The Qutub complex was partly built from the stone of earlier temples. That history is disputed and politically live, so it is left out; the facts stay with the minaret as a building.
- Kartavya Path's 2022 renaming is given as a date and a translation, with no comment.
- India Gate is a war memorial. Its fact says so plainly.

## Streets

### 1. Chandni Chowk: *the moonlit square*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1650**, laid out | Princess Jahanara, daughter of Emperor Shah Jahan, designed the market in 1650, leading up to the gates of the Red Fort. |
| *(none)* | Its name means 'moonlit square': a pool in the middle of the street once reflected the moon. |
| *(none)* | A canal once ran down the centre of the street. Today it is one of the biggest wholesale markets in India. |

### 2. Connaught Place: *rings of white colonnades*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1933**, completed | Built between 1929 and 1933, it was named after the Duke of Connaught, a son of Queen Victoria. |
| *(none)* | Architect Robert Tor Russell set its white colonnades in rings around a central park. |
| *(none)* | The design was modelled on the Royal Crescent, a curving terrace of houses in the English city of Bath. |

### 3. Kartavya Path: *the path of duty*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **2022**, renamed | First called Kingsway and then Rajpath, the avenue was renamed Kartavya Path, 'the path of duty', in 2022. |
| *(none)* | It runs from India Gate to Rashtrapati Bhavan, the President's house, between long lawns and canals. |
| *(none)* | Every year on 26 January, the Republic Day parade marches along it. |

## Monuments

### Red Fort, the Lahori Gate (no height graphic)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1648**, completed | Emperor Shah Jahan's fort took about nine years to build, from 1639 to 1648. |
| **2.4 km**, of walls | Its red sandstone walls run for about 2.4 kilometres, and give the Red Fort its name. |
| *(none)* | The main entrance, the Lahori Gate, is named after the city of Lahore, which it faces. |

Start date: sources give 1638 or 1639; "about nine years" covers both.

### Qutub Minar (scale: 72.5 m, compared with the Koutoubia Minaret; note: "Two great minarets of the 1190s." Koutoubia finished around 1195, Qutub begun 1199)

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **72.5 m**, tall | At 72.5 metres, it is the tallest minaret in the world built of brick. |
| **1199**, begun | Qutb ud-Din Aibak began the tower in 1199, and his successor Iltutmish added three more storeys. |
| *(none)* | It narrows from 14.3 metres across at the base to just 2.7 metres at the top. |

Some sources start it in 1192 or 1193; 1199 is the most common date.

### India Gate (scale: 42 m, compared with the Arc de Triomphe; note: "Both are memorial arches for soldiers.")

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **42 m**, tall | India Gate stands 42 metres high at the eastern end of Kartavya Path. |
| **1931**, unveiled | Designed by the architect Edwin Lutyens, it was begun in 1921 and unveiled in 1931. |
| *(none)* | It is a war memorial: the names of more than 13,000 soldiers of the Indian Army who died are carved into its stone. |

Names: Wikipedia gives 13,313 (some of them British). "More than 13,000" is safe.

## To check before approval

- "Named after the city of Lahore, which it faces": a common explanation, not found in the sources below.
- The Republic Day parade on Kartavya Path: well known, but not in the sources below.

## Copyright

The Red Fort (1639–48) and the Qutub Minar (from 1199) are centuries old. India Gate (1931) is by Edwin Lutyens, who died in 1944: out of copyright in India since 2005 (life + 60) and in the UK since 2015 (life + 70). The United States does not protect buildings completed before 1990.
