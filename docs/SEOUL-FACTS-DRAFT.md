# Seoul: facts for review

**DRAFT. Nothing here ships until Dan has approved it.** The facts are in `src/facts.js` already, marked DRAFT, so the city can be played and tested; the city is in the release queue and no player sees it.

3 streets × 3 facts, 3 monuments × 3 facts.

## Approach

- Facts are architectural and historical. The Japanese occupation is part of several of these stories (Insadong's antique trade; the palace's destruction and rebuilding). It is left out; the facts give dates only.
- **Monument change from the plan:** Sungnyemun was swapped for **Gyeonghoeru Pavilion**. Sungnyemun burned in 2008 and was rebuilt in 2013, which leaves its copyright position less clear than the others, and the three monuments would all have been gates or halls of the same shape. Gyeonghoeru, a banquet pavilion on 48 stone pillars in a pond, looks quite different.
- N Seoul Tower (1971) is left off the skyline: too recent.

## Monuments

No height graphic for any of the three: their facts carry the page.

### Heunginjimun Gate

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1869**, rebuilt | First built in 1398, the gate that stands today was rebuilt in 1869. |
| *(none)* | It is the only one of Seoul's great city gates guarded by an ongseong, a half-ring of wall built out in front of it. |
| **1962**, Treasure No. 1 | In 1962 South Korea listed it as Treasure No. 1. |

Sources give 1396 or 1398 for the first gate; the fact uses 1398. (Korea stopped numbering its treasures in 2021; the fact says "in 1962".)

### Gyeonghoeru Pavilion

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **48 pillars**, of stone | The pavilion stands on 48 stone pillars: square ones around the outside, round ones within. |
| **1867**, rebuilt | First built in 1412, it burned in 1592, leaving only its stone base, and was rebuilt in 1867. |
| *(none)* | Kings held banquets here and welcomed foreign envoys, out on its island in the palace pond. |

### Geunjeongjeon Hall

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|

| *(none)* | Stone animals, among them the twelve animals of the zodiac, guard the balustrades of its terraces. |
