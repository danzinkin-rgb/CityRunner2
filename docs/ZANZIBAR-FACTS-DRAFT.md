## Approach

- Facts are architectural and historical.
- **Christ Church stands on the site of the island's slave market.** The facts say so plainly, name the bishop who campaigned against slavery, and keep to what the sources state. The fact about the altar ("said to stand where the whipping post was") is the heaviest line in the city; it is your call whether a children's game says it. The slave memorial beside the cathedral (1998, by a living artist) is not shown.
- The 1896 war that damaged the House of Wonders is left out.

## Monuments

No sourced heights, so each shows altitude. Stone Town is on the shore; I found no sourced figure and used 1 m. Say if you want me to look harder, or show something else.

### The Old Fort

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1699**, built by | The fort was built by about 1699, after Oman took the island from Portugal, on the site of a Portuguese church. |
| *(none)* | It has served as a garrison, a prison and a railway terminus. |
| *(none)* | Today it is a cultural centre, and home to the Zanzibar International Film Festival. |

Dates vary (1698–1701, or the early 1710s); "by about 1699" follows Wikipedia and the 2022 excavation report.

### Christ Church Cathedral

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1873**, slave market closed | The cathedral stands where the island's slave market was, closed in 1873. Its first stone was laid that Christmas. |
| *(none)* | Bishop Edward Steere, who campaigned against slavery, led the building, and designed its unusual barrel-vaulted roof. |
| *(none)* | Its altar is said to stand where the whipping post was, so that the place of suffering became a place of worship. |

### House of Wonders

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1883**, built | Sultan Barghash built it in 1883 as a palace for ceremonies. |
| *(none)* | It was the first building in Zanzibar with electricity, and the first in East Africa with a lift: that is how it got its name. |
| *(none)* | Part of it collapsed in 2020, and it is being restored. |

"That is how it got its name" is the common explanation; check, or I will drop that clause.
