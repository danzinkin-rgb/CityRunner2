# Havana: facts for review

**Approved by Dan, 13 October 2026**, including the points under "To check before approval" below, which he checked and accepted. The facts are in `src/facts.js`; the city is in the release queue and no player sees it until it is released.

3 streets × 3 facts, 3 monuments × 3 facts.

## Approach

- Facts are architectural and historical. No politics, no revolution, no embargo.
- No cigars or rum, on signs or in facts (the game is rated 4+).
- The old cars are generic 1950s shapes with tail fins and chrome: no make's grille, badge or name.
- **Monument change from the plan:** El Capitolio (1929) was swapped for the **Castillo de la Real Fuerza** (1577). The Capitolio is a joint work by several architects, and Cuba protects such a work until 50 years after the last of its authors died. One of them, Eugenio Rayneri Piedra, died in 1960, but the death dates of the others (Raúl Otero among them) could not be confirmed.
- On US sanctions: the rules restrict business with Cuba, not depicting it. Nothing here involves any Cuban party.

## Streets

### 1. Malecón: *the sea wall*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1901**, begun | Building of the Malecón began in 1901, to protect the city from the sea. |
| **7 km**, of sea wall | It runs for about seven kilometres along the shore, built in stages over more than fifty years. |
| *(none)* | In rough weather, waves break right over the wall and across the road. |

Length: sources give 7.3 km (Cubanet, built in seven stages, finished 1958) and 8 km; the fact says "about seven".

### 2. Calle Obispo: *the bishop's street*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | Calle Obispo goes back to the 1500s, one of the oldest streets in Old Havana. |
| *(none)* | It has had many names, among them Pi Margall from 1905, but in 1936 it went back to Obispo, 'bishop'. |
| **1982**, World Heritage | Old Havana and its forts were listed by UNESCO in 1982. |

### 3. Paseo del Prado: *the promenade*

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1772**, begun | The Paseo del Prado was begun in 1772, a tree-lined promenade outside the old city walls. |
| *(none)* | Its raised walk down the middle is lined with trees, marble benches and bronze lions. |
| *(none)* | The lions were cast in 1928 from the metal of old cannons. |

The lions are not modelled in the game (they are sculptures); the facts only mention them.

## Monuments

No height graphic for any of the three: their facts carry the page.

### Castillo de la Real Fuerza

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1577**, completed | Completed in 1577, it is one of the oldest stone forts in the Americas. |
| *(none)* | The bronze weathervane on its tower, La Giraldilla, was cast in the 1630s and has become a symbol of Havana. |
| *(none)* | The Giraldilla on the tower today is a copy; the original is kept safe inside. |

### Havana Cathedral

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1777**, completed | Begun by the Jesuits in 1748, it was finished in 1777. |
| *(none)* | Its two bell towers are different widths, so that rainwater could drain from the square beside it. |
| **1789**, made a cathedral | It became Havana's cathedral in 1789. |

### Morro Castle

| Statistic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **1589**, begun | Work began in 1589, to a design by the Italian engineer Battista Antonelli. |
| *(none)* | In times of danger, an iron chain was stretched across the harbour mouth to the fort on the other side. |
| *(none)* | The lighthouse on its point was added in the 1800s, and still guides ships into the harbour. |

## To check before approval

- "Waves break right over the wall": widely reported, not in the sources below.
- "Still guides ships into the harbour": the lighthouse is working; not in the sources below.

## Copyright

All three monuments are Spanish colonial work of the 1500s to 1700s. The Prado's 1928 lions are not shown.
