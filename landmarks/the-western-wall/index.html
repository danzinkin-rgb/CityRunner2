The Western Wall facts

Jerusalem · 19 m tall
488m
total length

The Western Wall is the holiest place where Jews are permitted to pray, not the holiest site in Judaism — that is the Temple Mount above it. Only about 57 m of the wall is exposed at the prayer plaza; the rest runs on beneath later buildings.

19BCE
Herod's expansion

The wall is a retaining wall built to support the enlarged platform of the Second Temple, not a wall of the Temple itself.

1M+notes
placed every year

Written prayers are placed in the cracks. They are collected twice a year and buried on the Mount of Olives, never thrown away.

The street that leads there: Jaffa Road

The road to the sea

1860s
the first carriage road

Jaffa Road was cut in the 1860s as the first carriage road linking Jerusalem to the port of Jaffa, and pilgrims arriving by sea entered the city along it.

2011
reopened for light rail

Once choked with traffic, the street was rebuilt as a pedestrian and light-rail spine, reopening in 2011.

It runs from Jaffa Gate in the Old City wall out to the modern city — the seam where old Jerusalem meets new.
