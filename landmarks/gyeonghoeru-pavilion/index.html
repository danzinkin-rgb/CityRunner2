Gyeonghoeru Pavilion facts

Seoul
48pillars
of stone

The pavilion stands on 48 stone pillars: square ones around the outside, round ones within.

1867
rebuilt

First built in 1412, it burned in 1592, leaving only its stone base, and was rebuilt in 1867.

Kings held banquets here and welcomed foreign envoys, out on its island in the palace pond.

The street that leads there: Bukchon Hanok Village

Roofs of grey tile

Bukchon sits on the hill between two royal palaces, Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung.

Hundreds of hanok, traditional houses of timber and plaster under curved tile roofs, still stand in its lanes.

People still live here, so visitors are asked to keep their voices down as they walk through.
